# Cases

This file records practical examples of how the Nouscrates Oath is intended to apply.

These examples do not alter the canonical oath. If an example appears to conflict with `OATH.md`, the canonical oath controls.

## 1. AI can perform an existing employee's work

A competent person already has a role. Artificial intelligence becomes capable of performing most or all of that person's existing duties.

Dismissing that person solely because AI can now perform the work better is contrary to the oath.

Where an oath-taker has meaningful influence over the outcome, they should make a genuine effort to provide a reasonable alternative before displacement. This may include retraining, redeployment, time to develop another role, or other meaningful transition support.

The oath does not guarantee permanent employment regardless of circumstances. It concerns displacement whose sole reason is that AI can now perform the person's work better.

## 2. A person is unsuitable for reasons independent of AI

A person may be dismissed for legitimate reasons that would have justified dismissal even without artificial intelligence, such as persistent failure to perform required duties, serious misconduct, refusal to perform reasonable work, or another independent reason.

The oath does not protect a person from consequences unrelated to AI displacement.

The relevant question is whether the person's dismissal is occurring solely because AI can perform the work better.

## 3. AI is used instead of making a new hire

An organization has work to be done but has not yet entrusted that work to an existing person. It chooses to use artificial intelligence rather than create a new position or hire another person.

This does not violate the oath.

The oath does not create an obligation to create jobs, preserve hypothetical jobs, or employ a person merely because work could otherwise have been performed by one.

Its concern is the treatment of people whose existing livelihood is subsequently threatened by AI.

## 4. Automation reduces the amount of work available to an existing group

Automation substantially reduces the amount of routine work performed by a group of otherwise competent people.

A response consistent with the oath is to make a genuine effort to create alternative paths before dismissing people solely because their previous duties have diminished.

Those paths might include retraining, movement into different functions, participation in new projects, or opportunities to develop work that becomes possible because of the additional capacity created by automation.

## 5. A transition program for people affected by automation

An organization may establish a program specifically intended to help people whose existing duties are being reduced by AI.

Such a program might provide time, training, tools, mentoring, experimental assignments, or opportunities to develop new roles.

This is consistent with the oath when its purpose is to create genuine alternatives to displacement rather than merely delay a predetermined dismissal.

Participation may be voluntary where appropriate.

## 6. Experimental opportunities during retraining

A person moving into a substantially different role may initially lack the experience expected of someone already established in that role.

An organization may therefore create appropriately supervised experimental opportunities with expectations suited to learning and transition.

This does not require lowering safety, honesty, or essential quality standards.

The purpose is to give a competent person a realistic opportunity to become useful in a new capacity rather than requiring immediate mastery of an unfamiliar profession.

## 7. Knowledge already belongs to an institution or is already public

A person may possess AI knowledge that is already recorded in institutional systems, required to be retained, publicly available, or otherwise outside that person's control.

The oath does not require the person to delete, conceal, suppress, or prevent access to information they do not control.

It also does not require violation of law, professional duties, contractual obligations, records requirements, or other legitimate obligations.

The oath governs the person's own voluntary actions where meaningful discretion exists.

## 8. Personally teaching powerful automation methods

An oath-taker possesses knowledge that could materially increase another person's ability to replace existing workers with AI.

Where sharing that knowledge is voluntary, the oath-taker should not knowingly teach, promote, elaborate upon, or actively transmit it to another person unless that person freely accepts the same canonical oath and assumes the same responsibility regarding further transmission.

The distinction is between information that exists independently and the oath-taker's own voluntary contribution to spreading or amplifying it.

## 9. Publicly available knowledge versus active promotion

The fact that information can already be found somewhere does not require an oath-taker to promote it.

An oath-taker may refrain from giving tutorials, demonstrations, presentations, advocacy, detailed assistance, or other voluntary help that would materially facilitate displacement, even when some underlying information is publicly available.

The oath does not require censorship.

It governs the oath-taker's own voluntary participation in propagation.

## 10. Required disclosure or required professional work

A person's lawfully imposed duties may require disclosure of information or performance of work involving AI.

The oath does not require violation of such legitimate obligations.

Its restriction on propagation applies where the person has meaningful discretion over whether to teach, promote, elaborate upon, or actively transmit the relevant knowledge.

## 11. A person with authority over staffing

Someone with meaningful authority over employment decisions has responsibilities under the oath that a person without such authority may not have.

If AI makes an otherwise competent person's existing duties unnecessary, the oath-taker should make a genuine effort to identify reasonable alternatives before causing that person's dismissal solely because of AI.

Possible alternatives include retraining, redeployment, a reasonable transition period, trial work in another function, or other meaningful support.

The oath does not require indefinite employment when reasonable alternatives have genuinely failed or do not exist.

## 12. A person without authority over staffing

An oath-taker does not violate the oath merely because another person or organization makes an employment decision that the oath-taker cannot control.

Responsibility extends to the oath-taker's own conduct: what they advocate, what they facilitate, what knowledge they voluntarily propagate, and whatever influence they genuinely possess.

The oath does not assign responsibility for actions outside a person's practical and moral control.

## 13. Applying a stricter standard to oneself

An oath-taker may voluntarily accept consequences for themselves that they would not consider morally permissible to impose upon another person.

For example, a person might personally be willing to surrender their own role if AI can perform it better while refusing to advocate the dismissal of another otherwise competent person on that ground alone.

The oath establishes obligations concerning how others are treated.

It does not prevent a person from demanding more of themselves.

## 14. AI improves productivity without displacing anyone

AI may be used to increase productivity, improve quality, automate tedious work, discover new possibilities, create new products or services, or accomplish work that otherwise would not be performed.

Where no otherwise competent person's existing livelihood is threatened solely because of that automation, there is no conflict with the oath.

Nouscrates is not an anti-AI or anti-automation pledge.

## 15. Automation creates new forms of useful work

AI may eliminate some existing duties while simultaneously making new activities, products, services, or responsibilities possible.

Using some of the resulting capacity to help existing people move toward those new opportunities is consistent with the oath and represents one of the outcomes it is intended to encourage.

## 16. No reasonable transition is possible

There may be circumstances in which genuine efforts at retraining, redeployment, or other meaningful transition support fail, are refused, or no reasonable alternative exists.

The oath does not promise that every employment relationship can continue indefinitely.

Its requirement is that an otherwise competent person not be treated as disposable solely because AI became better at their existing task, without a genuine effort to seek a humane alternative where the oath-taker has meaningful power to do so.
